Phase 1

Motivating Introduction & Theoretical Foundations

Time: ~15 Minutes

1. Warm-Up Activity: "What makes people different?" (5 mins)

Objective: Activate prior knowledge and encourage self-reflection on human differences.

Interactive Prompt for the Class:

"Think of two siblings raised in the exact same household. One is loud, impulsive, and loves crowds; the other is quiet, deliberate, and prefers reading alone. Why are they so different?"

Genetics? Upbringing? Personal choices?

2. Direct Instruction: Key Concepts (10 mins)

Personality

The unique and relatively stable pattern of thoughts, feelings, and behaviors that characterizes an individual over time and across situations.

Blends biological tendencies & life experiences.
Temperament

The biologically based, innate emotional reactivity and arousal disposition present from infancy (e.g., reactivity to noise, mood baseline).

Highly genetic / Biological nature.
Character

The moral and ethical values, self-discipline, and socialized traits built over time through cultural values, choice, and habits.

Learned / Moral nurture & choices.
Phase 2

Individual Work: Examples vs. Non-Examples Analysis

Time: ~15 Minutes

Instructions: Read the following behavioral scenarios in your notebook. Classify whether each scenario describes Temperament, Character, or General Personality. Click the buttons below each card to verify your answer!

Case Scenario A

"A newborn baby cries intensely whenever there is a sudden light change and sleeps irregularly, showing high sensory reactivity from day one."

Answer: Temperament.
Why? It is an innate biological response present at birth without prior moral training or cultural learning.
Case Scenario B

"Sophia chooses to return a lost wallet with $100 intact to the owner because of her strong commitment to honesty and integrity."

Answer: Character.
Why? It reflects moral decision-making, ethical values, and self-discipline developed through life choices.
Case Scenario C

"Carlos is generally extraverted, organized in his schoolwork, empathetic towards friends, and stays calm during high-stress exams."

Answer: Personality.
Why? It represents an overarching, consistent pattern of thoughts, emotional styles, and social behaviors across various settings.
Case Scenario D

"Mateo experiences a sudden surge of adrenaline and quickened heartbeat when spoken to loudly."

Answer: Temperament (Physiological Reactivity).
Why? It relates directly to automatic physiological arousal and biological sensitivity.
Phase 3

Cooperative Activity: Nature vs. Nurture Continuum

Time: ~15 Minutes

Team Challenge Instructions (Teams of 4):

Collaborate with your team to plot human psychological traits onto the Nature vs. Nurture Continuum Matrix below. Assign roles: Leader (Habit 1), Scribe, Timekeeper, and Spokesperson (Habit 5).

Nature-Nurture Continuum Scale

100% NATURE (Genetics/Biology) 50/50 INTERACTION 100% NURTURE (Environment/Culture)
Predominantly Nature
  • Activity level as an infant
  • Introversion/Extraversion baseline
  • Sensory sensitivity thresholds
Nature + Nurture Interaction
  • Emotional self-regulation
  • Anxiety disposition
  • Social leadership style
Predominantly Nurture
  • Religious and moral values
  • Table manners & etiquette
  • Political ideology

Team Discussion Question:

"Can a person change their fundamental personality over time? What changes easily (character/habits) and what stays resistant (temperament)?"
